## Purpose
This controlled source document defines NexaFlow Technologies requirements for data retention and deletion. It is designed to give a clear company reference for lawful and proportionate handling of personal data throughout its lifecycle. The document is intentionally explicit about what is required, what is merely permitted, where approval is needed, and how uncertainty should be handled so that employees and company systems do not invent policy details that are not supported by an approved source.

## Scope
This document applies to employees, service owners, Privacy, and approved processors whenever work falls within the data retention and deletion domain. It should be read together with relevant security, privacy, contractual, employment, financial, product, and legal requirements. A customer contract or applicable law may impose an additional or stricter requirement for a specific situation. When that happens, the stricter or specifically applicable requirement should be followed without rewriting the baseline NexaFlow policy for unrelated cases.

## Policy and operating information
1. Business records are retained according to an approved retention schedule.
2. Data should not be retained indefinitely without a documented business, legal, security, or contractual reason.
3. Expired records should be deleted or anonymized when no preservation obligation applies.
4. Legal holds override normal deletion schedules for the affected records.
5. Customer deletion requests are processed according to applicable contract, product capability, and legal requirements.
6. Backups may retain deleted information for a limited period until normal backup expiration.
7. Retention requirements should be documented for systems storing Confidential or Restricted information.
8. System owners are responsible for implementing applicable retention and deletion controls.
9. Data Retention and Deletion activities must follow documented NexaFlow procedures and applicable legal, contractual, security, and privacy requirements.
10. Privacy owns the baseline standard for data retention and deletion and reviews material exceptions.
11. Requests or changes related to data retention and deletion should be recorded when they affect customers, company data, access, money, employment, or production services.
12. Personnel must use least-privilege access and only information reasonably required for data retention and deletion activities.
13. Material decisions involving data retention and deletion require an accountable owner and enough documentation for later review.
14. Exceptions to the normal data retention and deletion process require documented approval unless an emergency process applies.
15. Sensitive information encountered during data retention and deletion must follow data-classification and access-control requirements.
16. Where a customer contract specifies stricter data retention and deletion requirements, approved contractual terms take precedence for that customer.
17. Records created through data retention and deletion should identify the request, owner, date, approvals, and outcome where relevant.
18. The responsible function periodically reviews data retention and deletion procedures for effectiveness.

## Records and evidence
Records created for data retention and deletion should be accurate, attributable, and proportionate to the risk and importance of the activity. A useful record normally identifies what was requested or observed, the relevant decision, the responsible owner, required approval where applicable, and any follow-up action. Records must not be falsified, selectively altered, or removed in order to create a misleading history. Sensitive records remain subject to access-control, classification, privacy, and retention requirements.

## Exceptions and escalation
A material exception to the normal data retention and deletion process requires authorization from Privacy or another policy owner who is explicitly empowered to approve the exception. The record should state the reason, scope, duration where relevant, and any compensating action. Urgency, seniority, customer pressure, convenience, or a verbal statement from an unrelated person does not by itself establish an exception. If the policy is silent or two controlled sources appear to conflict, the matter should be escalated rather than resolved by guessing.
