## Purpose
This controlled source document defines NexaFlow Technologies requirements for software and acceptable use. It is designed to give a clear company reference for reliable and secure use of corporate technology, devices, software, identities, and support services. The document is intentionally explicit about what is required, what is merely permitted, where approval is needed, and how uncertainty should be handled so that employees and company systems do not invent policy details that are not supported by an approved source.

## Scope
This document applies to employees, contractors, managers, and IT whenever work falls within the software and acceptable use domain. It should be read together with relevant security, privacy, contractual, employment, financial, product, and legal requirements. A customer contract or applicable law may impose an additional or stricter requirement for a specific situation. When that happens, the stricter or specifically applicable requirement should be followed without rewriting the baseline NexaFlow policy for unrelated cases.

## Policy and operating information
1. Software used to process confidential NexaFlow or customer data must be approved for the intended use.
2. Employees may request new software through the Corporate IT approval process.
3. Employees must not intentionally install malware, credential-stealing tools, or software intended to bypass company security controls.
4. Personal use of company devices must remain limited and must not interfere with work, security, or legal obligations.
5. Company credentials must not be shared with another person.
6. Unauthorized cryptocurrency mining is prohibited on NexaFlow computing resources.
7. Peer-to-peer file-sharing software is prohibited on company-managed devices unless specifically approved for a documented business purpose.
8. Corporate IT may remove software that creates an unacceptable security or compliance risk.
9. Software and Acceptable Use activities must follow documented NexaFlow procedures and applicable legal, contractual, security, and privacy requirements.
10. Corporate IT owns the baseline standard for software and acceptable use and reviews material exceptions.
11. Requests or changes related to software and acceptable use should be recorded when they affect customers, company data, access, money, employment, or production services.
12. Personnel must use least-privilege access and only information reasonably required for software and acceptable use activities.
13. Material decisions involving software and acceptable use require an accountable owner and enough documentation for later review.
14. Exceptions to the normal software and acceptable use process require documented approval unless an emergency process applies.
15. Sensitive information encountered during software and acceptable use must follow data-classification and access-control requirements.
16. Where a customer contract specifies stricter software and acceptable use requirements, approved contractual terms take precedence for that customer.
17. Records created through software and acceptable use should identify the request, owner, date, approvals, and outcome where relevant.
18. The responsible function periodically reviews software and acceptable use procedures for effectiveness.
